Ather battery data from real owners

State of Health, odometer and cell imbalance submitted by Ather owners — so the numbers people compare against come from actual scooters, not marketing.

Self-reported  ·  Anonymous by default  ·  Updated by hand, no tracking

Every owner wants to know the same thing: is my battery normal for its age? The honest answer is that nobody can tell you without data — and the data that exists publicly is almost all anecdote. This page is an attempt to fix that, one owner at a time.

Submit your numbers once, anonymously if you prefer, and they appear here alongside everyone else’s. Three fields do most of the work: State of Health, odometer and cell delta.

Note: these are self-reported readings from owners using their own reader. That is the point — it reflects real scooters in real conditions — but treat individual rows as claims, not measurements. The pattern across many rows is what is useful.

So far 5 owners have submitted a reading: median SOH 88.3% and median cell delta 1.2 mV.

Battery health vs odometer

Each dot is one submission. The shape to look for is the slope: a gentle decline is normal wear, a steep one at low mileage is worth investigating.

Cell imbalance vs odometer

Cell delta is measured in millivolts (mV) — the gap between the strongest and weakest cell group, which is why it starts small. It tends to drift upward with age and mileage. A tight cluster with a few high outliers usually means individual weak packs rather than a fleet-wide problem.

How this data is collected, and what it cannot tell you

Self-reported. Owners read their own dashboards. A typo or an optimistic reading is possible, and there is no way to audit a submission. The values are shown as sent, with light range checks that drop obvious nonsense (SOH outside 0–100, odometer beyond six figures).

One point in time. Each row is a snapshot, not a degradation curve. The same scooter can appear twice at different mileages, which is useful — but rows are submissions, not unique scooters.

Download it if you want it. The whole dataset is a single public JSON file — community-scooters.json — so anyone can check the charts, run their own analysis or cite the numbers without asking.

Not a warranty opinion. Nothing here decides whether Ather will honour a claim. If your pack reads below 70% SOH inside the cover period, the warranty section of the guide explains what evidence to gather.

Why cell delta is included. SOH tells you how much capacity is left; cell delta tells you whether the pack is still working evenly. Two scooters can share an SOH and be in very different health. If you only send one number, send cell delta.

Charge level matters. Both SOH and cell delta read slightly differently depending on state of charge, so the form asks for SoC at the time of reading. It is there to keep comparisons honest, not to score anyone.

Ather battery data — common questions

There is no published benchmark, which is exactly the gap this page fills. What can be said without guessing: a new pack reads 100%, Ather’s battery warranty is written around 70%, and a pack still in the 80s after a few years of use is unremarkable. The useful comparison is your number against submissions at a similar age and mileage — not against one headline figure.

Usually longer than the warranty period, but “last” needs unpacking. Capacity declines gradually rather than failing on a date, and a pack stops being practical when its range no longer covers your rides. Two scooters of the same age can differ by ten percentage points or more depending on climate, charging habit and how hard they were ridden, so the age-band medians above are more useful than any single lifespan claim.

Readings are in millivolts, and a healthy pack sits within tens of millivolts — the exact spread moves with charge level and temperature, so compare readings taken at a similar SoC. A gap that keeps growing between checks, or one cell group sitting clearly lower than the rest, points at a weak group rather than general wear. That is the case worth a service centre visit; a stable number in the tens is not.

The measurement comes from the scooter’s own battery management system, so the number itself is real; what is not verified is that the row was typed in honestly and without errors. That is why this page shows medians, age bands and a scatter with every outlier visible instead of a single flattering average, and why obvious nonsense is dropped on import.
